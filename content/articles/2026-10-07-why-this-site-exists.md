I work as a business analyst in payments. I can read a payment ticket, trace a transaction through our systems, and write a requirement that a developer can build. What I want is the next level: to understand the *whole* domain well enough that I can explain any part of it from first principles, spot problems before they become incidents, and be the person in the room who knows why things are the way they are.

So I built a course for myself from free resources — regulator FAQs, Swift's own pages, bank implementation guides, the ISO 20022 catalogue — and organised it into twelve modules that build on each other. It starts with "what is clearing?" and ends with exceptions and investigations in ISO 20022, payment-hub architecture, and the BA skills that wrap around all of it.

## Why in public

Three reasons.

1. **Writing is the test.** If I can't explain serial versus cover payments to someone who has never seen an MT202 COV, I don't understand it yet.
2. **A portfolio beats a claim.** "I know ISO 20022" is a line on a CV. Twelve articles walking through pacs.008, camt.056 and camt.029 are evidence.
3. **Someone else is where I was.** Most payments knowledge sits behind logins and paid courses. The free material exists, but it is scattered. Collecting it in one place might save the next person a few months.

## How to use it

Start at [module one](../../course/m1/) and go in order. Each module has resources in reading order, an exercise, and a checkpoint question. Do the exercise — reading alone doesn't stick. If you only have ten minutes, read the checkpoint and see whether you can answer it.

I'll add an article every couple of weeks as I finish modules. The first proper one is on the four-corner model and why a cheque takes two days while UPI takes two seconds.
