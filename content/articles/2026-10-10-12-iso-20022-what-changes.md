Every earlier part mentioned message names in passing — `pacs.008`, `camt.056`, MT103. This final part explains the standard they come from, using one payment written both ways. If you have followed the series, the message families will slot straight onto the [four-corner model](../2026-10-10-03-four-corner-model-parties/).

## What ISO 20022 is

ISO 20022 is an international standard for financial messages. Three layers:

1. **A business model** — a shared dictionary of concepts (debtor, creditor, agent, remittance information, settlement date) with precise definitions, so "beneficiary" means the same thing in Mumbai and Frankfurt.
2. **Message definitions** — specific messages built from that dictionary, each with a name like `pacs.008.001.xx` (business area . message . variant . version).
3. **A syntax** — today XML; the standard allows others, and JSON profiles exist for APIs.

The standard is maintained by ISO with Swift as registration authority, but it is not a Swift product: UPI's successor specs, the Eurosystem's T2, Fedwire, CHIPS, the Bank of England's RTGS, SEPA and dozens of other systems use it with no Swift involvement. What Swift does own is **CBPR+** — the usage guidelines that say how the generic messages are used for cross-border payments on its network. Each market has its own guidelines the same way (HVPS+ for high-value systems, the EPC implementation guidelines for SEPA).

## The three families, placed on the model

```
                 pain.*                                      camt.*
  Payer ───────────────────▶ Payer's PSP                Payee's PSP ───────────────▶ Payee
  (initiation: pain.001,                                  (reporting: camt.052/053/054,
   status: pain.002)              │                      │   investigations: camt.056/029)
                                  └────── pacs.* ────────┘
                                   (clearing & settlement:
                                    pacs.008, 009, 004, 002)
```

- **pain — payment initiation.** Customer to bank. `pain.001` is a credit transfer initiation (a corporate's payment file, formerly MT101); `pain.008` a direct debit initiation; `pain.002` the status back to the customer.
- **pacs — payment clearing and settlement.** Bank to bank. `pacs.008` customer credit transfer (MT103); `pacs.009` financial-institution transfer (MT202), with the COV variant from [part 9](../2026-10-10-09-correspondent-banking-serial-cover/); `pacs.003` direct debit collection; `pacs.004` return; `pacs.002` status report (accept, reject, pending); `pacs.007` reversal.
- **camt — cash management.** Bank to customer and bank to bank about accounts and exceptions. `camt.053` end-of-day statement (MT940/950); `camt.052` intraday (MT942); `camt.054` debit/credit notification (MT900/910); `camt.056` cancellation request (MT192); `camt.029` resolution of investigation (MT196).

The mnemonic: **pain** is what the customer feels, **pacs** is the pipe, **camt** is the paperwork afterwards.

## The same payment, twice

Priya at Bank X in Mumbai pays USD 1,200 to Rahul at Chase for an invoice. Here is the heart of the instruction in both formats (abbreviated; real messages carry more).

**As an MT103 (the old way)**

```
:20:BX2026101000123
:23B:CRED
:32A:261010USD1200,
:50K:/0012345678
PRIYA SHARMA
14 MARINE DRIVE MUMBAI 400020 INDIA
:52A:BANKINBBXXX
:57A:CHASUS33XXX
:59:/987654321
RAHUL MEHTA
220 EAST 42ND ST NEW YORK NY 10017 US
:70:INV 4471 CONSULTING SEP 2026
:71A:SHA
```

Fields are numbered tags. Names and addresses are up to four lines of 35 free-text characters. A screening system has to *guess* which line is the street and which is the country. The remittance information is 140 characters of whatever the payer typed.

**As a pacs.008 (ISO 20022, abbreviated)**

```xml
<CdtTrfTxInf>
  <PmtId>
    <InstrId>BX2026101000123</InstrId>
    <EndToEndId>INV4471</EndToEndId>
    <UETR>eb6305c9-1f7f-49de-aed0-16487c27b42d</UETR>
  </PmtId>
  <IntrBkSttlmAmt Ccy="USD">1200.00</IntrBkSttlmAmt>
  <IntrBkSttlmDt>2026-10-10</IntrBkSttlmDt>
  <ChrgBr>SHAR</ChrgBr>
  <Dbtr>
    <Nm>Priya Sharma</Nm>
    <PstlAdr>
      <StrtNm>Marine Drive</StrtNm><BldgNb>14</BldgNb>
      <PstCd>400020</PstCd><TwnNm>Mumbai</TwnNm><Ctry>IN</Ctry>
    </PstlAdr>
  </Dbtr>
  <DbtrAcct><Id><Othr><Id>0012345678</Id></Othr></Id></DbtrAcct>
  <DbtrAgt><FinInstnId><BICFI>BANKINBBXXX</BICFI></FinInstnId></DbtrAgt>
  <CdtrAgt><FinInstnId><BICFI>CHASUS33XXX</BICFI></FinInstnId></CdtrAgt>
  <Cdtr>
    <Nm>Rahul Mehta</Nm>
    <PstlAdr>
      <StrtNm>East 42nd Street</StrtNm><BldgNb>220</BldgNb>
      <PstCd>10017</PstCd><TwnNm>New York</TwnNm>
      <CtrySubDvsn>NY</CtrySubDvsn><Ctry>US</Ctry>
    </PstlAdr>
  </Cdtr>
  <CdtrAcct><Id><Othr><Id>987654321</Id></Othr></Id></CdtrAcct>
  <Purp><Cd>SCVE</Cd></Purp>
  <RmtInf>
    <Strd>
      <RfrdDocInf><Tp><CdOrPrtry><Cd>CINV</Cd></CdOrPrtry></Tp><Nb>4471</Nb></RfrdDocInf>
      <RfrdDocAmt><DuePyblAmt Ccy="USD">1200.00</DuePyblAmt></RfrdDocAmt>
    </Strd>
  </RmtInf>
</CdtTrfTxInf>
```

Longer, obviously. But look at what it *knows* that the MT103 didn't:

| Aspect | MT103 | pacs.008 |
|---|---|---|
| Address | Four free-text lines | Separate street, number, postcode, town, region, country |
| Purpose | Implied in free text | A coded purpose (`SCVE` = purchase of services) from an external list |
| Remittance | 140 characters of text | Structured: document type (commercial invoice), number, amount — a machine can auto-reconcile it |
| Identifiers | Reference numbers only | Room for LEI, tax IDs, dates of birth, national IDs where required |
| Tracking | UETR in a header block | UETR as a first-class element |
| Character set | Latin, restricted | Full Unicode where guidelines allow |

That table is what people mean by "richer data". The practical consequences:

- **Sanctions screening gets more precise.** Screening a `<Ctry>` field against a country list is exact; screening a free-text line that might contain "Cuba Street" is not. False positives fall. (Though more fields also means more to screen, which is why early migrations saw hit rates go *up* before tuning.)
- **Reconciliation can be automated.** The invoice number is in a tagged field, not somewhere in a sentence.
- **Truncation becomes the migration risk.** A `pacs.008` with 300 characters of structured remittance that has to be converted to a 140-character MT field loses data. During coexistence this was the number-one operational issue, and it is why systems still holding MT formats internally (channels, core banking, archives) needed care.

## Where things stand, and the dates still ahead

- **22 November 2025** — end of MT/MX coexistence for cross-border payment *instructions* on Swift. `pacs.008`, `pacs.009`, `pacs.004` and `pacs.002` are mandatory; MT103, MT202 and friends are no longer accepted for CBPR+ traffic.
- **November 2026** — unstructured postal addresses retired across CBPR+ and the EPC schemes; structured or hybrid only. If a counterparty still sends a free-text address, expect rejects.
- **November 2026 onward** — payment cancellations (`camt.056`/`camt.029`) must flow through Swift's Case Management tooling; all institutions must be able to receive the new investigation message `camt.110`.
- **November 2027** — legacy MT192/195/196 investigation messages are retired; `camt.110`/`camt.111` are the only route.
- **2027–2028** — the remaining families: statements and reporting (MT 9xx → `camt.052/053/054`), cheques, direct debits.

Domestic systems have mostly already moved: India's RTGS (2013), the Eurosystem's T2 (March 2023), CHIPS (April 2024) and Fedwire (July 2025).

## How to read any message from here

You now have everything the series set out to give you. Faced with an unfamiliar ISO 20022 message:

1. Read the prefix — pain, pacs or camt — and you know which [edge of the four-corner model](../2026-10-10-03-four-corner-model-parties/) it travels on.
2. Find the `UETR` and the agent BICs ([part 10](../2026-10-10-10-swift-what-it-is/)) and you can place it in a chain ([part 9](../2026-10-10-09-correspondent-banking-serial-cover/)).
3. Check the settlement amount and date and you know which [entries](../2026-10-10-06-accounting-entries-end-to-end/) each bank should have made.
4. If it's a `pacs.004`, `pacs.002` or `camt.056`, look at the reason code and the [R-transaction vocabulary](../2026-10-10-11-sepa-sct-inst-direct-debit/) tells you what went wrong and who owes whom.

That is the whole domain, in one loop: an instrument, pushed or pulled, across four corners, cleared and settled, booked on ledgers, carried by messages that now say exactly what they mean.
